# Closing the Dec-26 UK−EUR policy-pricing trade at the first sub-4 bp print

*Closes [Long BoE vs ECB Dec-26 @ +4.5bps](../post_17/body.md), opened 28 July 2026 with the proceeds of the [Mar-27/Mar-28 SOFR steepener](../post_16/body.md). Short **JUZ26** (ICE 1M SONIA Dec-26) / long **IJZ26** (ICE 1M €STR Dec-26), $25/bp on $1m face per leg. Entry 28 Jul 2026 EOD, exit **30 Jul 2026 EOD**. Barchart ICE EOD; policy anchors BoE Bank Rate 3.75%, ECB deposit facility 2.25%.*

The position was never a view on the 150 bp of level between UK and euro-area front-end rates. Quote each leg against its own policy anchor and what is left is a bet on one number: how much more cumulative policy change the market prices from the Bank of England than from the ECB by the end of 2026.

At entry that number was **+4.5 bp**. SONIA Dec-26 printed 4.040%, or +29.0 bp above a Bank Rate of 3.75%. €STR Dec-26 printed 2.495%, or +24.5 bp above a deposit facility of 2.25%. The UK was priced to deliver 4.5 bp more tightening by December than the euro area, on the entry claim that the BoE had been immeasurably more dovish in its guidance than the ECB and was operating against a weaker macro backdrop and a policy rate further above neutral.

The published plan was target **−4.5 bp**, stop **+8.5 bp**. This close does not wait for that target. It takes the first end-of-day print where the relative sits **at or below +4 bp** — a near-parity trigger, and deliberately so. What the entry note actually argued was a divergence in *guidance*, and the strong form of that claim is satisfied the moment the market stops pricing the BoE to out-tighten the ECB at all. Convergence to parity is the thesis being realised; everything past parity is a directional bet that the euro area out-tightens the UK, which is a different trade and needs its own underwriting. The cost of that discipline is quantified at the end.

It triggered on the second session.

## Day one went the wrong way

The first session after entry cost 1.5 bp on the relative and $37.50 of mark. The UK leg added 4.5 bp of priced tightening to +33.5 bp into the meeting, against 3.0 bp on the euro leg. That is the whole risk of putting on a near-parity convergence trade two days before a central bank decision: there is no room, so ordinary pre-meeting risk premium is immediately adverse.

## The MPC did the work in one session, and not through the vote

The Committee voted **6–3 to maintain Bank Rate at 3.75%** at the meeting ending 29 July, announced 30 July. Megan Greene, Catherine Mann and Huw Pill preferred +25 bp to 4.00%. That was one dissenter more than June and one more than the 7–2 split economists polled by Reuters had expected, so on the vote count alone the meeting was a hawkish surprise.

The front end rallied anyway, because everything around the vote was dovish. The majority's stated reasoning was that holding, "combined with the significant tightening of financial conditions that had occurred since the conflict started, was providing sufficient insurance against the upside risks to inflation stemming from fluctuations in energy prices," and the Bank saw few signs of the Iran oil shock propagating through the domestic economy. Bailey then removed the remaining ambiguity at the press conference: *"I don't want you to leave this room thinking that the BOE is leaning toward raising rates — frankly, there's nothing in what I've said or what any of us have said that suggests that."*

The two-year gilt yield fell 12.3 bp to 4.34%, its largest one-day drop in more than two months, and LSEG data had tightening priced for the remainder of 2026 cut from 38 bp to 29 bp. Our leg agrees: JUZ26 went from +33.5 bp to +26.0 bp above Bank Rate, a 7.5 bp dovish repricing in a single session. An extra dissenter is worth a few basis points in the front contract; a governor explicitly disowning the hiking path is worth more, and the two did not net out anywhere near zero.

The euro leg did almost nothing. €STR Dec-26 moved from +27.5 bp to +26.0 bp above the deposit rate, −1.5 bp. So 6.0 bp of the 7.5 bp of UK repricing dropped straight through to the relative, which closed at exactly parity — the first print at or below the +4 bp trigger, and it cleared it by four basis points.

Worth separating out what did *not* drive this. The Fed held on 29 July and short-dated US yields fell overnight, which helped pull gilt yields lower into the London morning. That is a level effect largely common to both legs and it does not survive the relative. The move that paid was specific to the Bank of England's own reaction function.

Two sessions held, maximum adverse mark −$37.50. Note that the relative and absolute changes are identical to the basis point: both policy rates were unchanged across the window, so the 150 bp policy gap is a constant and drops out of the difference. That equality is not a general property of the trade — it is what makes the relative framing safe to use as the P&L metric only so long as neither central bank moves inside the holding period.

## What the rule cost

The relative did not stop at parity. It kept grinding lower and the published **−4.5 bp target printed exactly, on 4 August** — six sessions after this close. It went on to **−9.0 bp on 4 September**.

| Exit | Date | Relative | P&L |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Sub-4 bp parity rule (taken) | 30 Jul | 0.0 bp | +4.5 bp / **+$112.50** |
| Published −4.5 bp target | 4 Aug | −4.5 bp | +9.0 bp / +$225.00 |
| Last mark | 4 Sep | −9.0 bp | +13.5 bp / +$337.50 |

So the parity rule banked half of what the stated target would have paid and a third of the eventual peak. The stop was never threatened: the relative's high-water mark was +6.0 bp on 29 July against a +8.5 bp stop.

That is the honest accounting, and the trade-off is still the right one to have made. The move from parity to −4.5 bp was not the convergence thesis paying off twice; it was the market moving on to price the euro area out-tightening the UK, which is a claim the entry note never made. Nor would the extra five weeks have been a comfortable hold. The path from parity to −9 bp reversed against the position by 2.5 bp twice. The first was 28 August, when Warsh's Jackson Hole keynote lifted global front ends: the UK leg added 2.5 bp of priced tightening while the euro leg sat still at +26.5 bp. The second was 1 September, UK +3.0 bp against euro +0.5 bp. A position entered with half a basis point of room between the market and the thesis has no business sitting through 2.5 bp reversals.

## Book

Entry was funded by the $212.50 of take-profit proceeds from the Mar-27/Mar-28 3M SOFR steepener closed on 28 July. Adding $112.50 takes cumulative realised P&L across the two rolls to **$325.00**. The book is now flat.

The convergence trade re-arms if the relative returns to meaningfully positive — the UK once again priced to out-tighten the euro area through Dec-26 by more than a few basis points. As of the last print it is 9 bp the other way, and the asymmetry now sits in the euro leg: on 27 August, LSEG had roughly 24 bp of tightening priced into the ECB's 10 September decision against under 4 bp for the BoE's 17 September meeting. Whatever the next expression of this pair is, it is not short the UK against Europe.
