### The type of flattening

This was a **bull flattening**, and an unusually lopsided one. Both legs richened — implied rates fell on each — so the direction is unambiguously bull rather than bear. The flattening came from the UK leg doing effectively all of the work: **−10.5 bp on Dec-26 SONIA against just −1.5 bp on Dec-26 €STR**. Had the euro leg not richened at all, the spread would have compressed by 10.5 bp rather than 9.0; the long €STR leg gave back 1.5 bp of the move.

That composition is the point of the trade. We were not paid because global front ends rallied — we were paid because the UK rallied roughly seven times harder than the euro area did while doing so. Priced BoE tightening through December fell from +29.0bps to +18.5bps, a 36% reduction in the whole hiking path in five sessions, while priced ECB tightening barely moved, from +24.5bps to +23.0bps.

---

**Reason for entering trade:** BoE end-2026 pricing at +29.0bps was more hawkish than the ECB's +24.5bps despite the BoE having been immeasurably more dovish in its guidance — Bailey had already suggested the market's own repricing had done much of the Bank's work for it — and despite the BoE operating against a weaker macro backdrop with a policy rate further above neutral. The ECB, by contrast, had actually delivered a hike in June before holding on 23 July. The trade was also constructed to be orthogonal to energy prices: whether the oil shock intensified or faded, BoE pricing should sell off less or rally more than ECB pricing.

**What happened:** the front end bull flattened, and the catalyst was guidance rather than the vote. On 30 July the MPC held at 3.75% by 6−3, with Greene, Mann and Pill dissenting for +25bps. That was one dissenter *more* than the 7−2 split consensus had looked for, so on the vote count the meeting was a hawkish surprise. It did not matter. The majority framed the hold as sufficient insurance given how much financial conditions had already tightened, the Bank saw few signs of the oil shock propagating domestically, and Bailey shut the door explicitly: *"I don't want you to leave this room thinking that the BOE is leaning toward raising rates — frankly, there's nothing in what I've said or what any of us have said that suggests that."* The 2-year gilt fell 12.3bps to 4.34%, its largest one-day drop in over two months, and priced tightening for the remainder of 2026 was cut from 38bps to 29bps. Our UK leg richened 7.5bps that session alone; the euro leg gave back 1.5bps.

The follow-through over 3−4 August came from the same direction. UK and euro-area final manufacturing PMIs were both revised lower on the Monday, with UK factory activity showing the larger correction, and the sell-side began fading the post-MPC sterling rally on the view that — the vote split aside — the rest of the committee remained comfortable on hold given the absence of clear second-round effects, with further paring of September BoE hike pricing to come. Both legs richened again into the Tuesday on an otherwise empty calendar, the UK by more, and the relative touched −4.5bps.

The position is closed and the book is flat. Realised P&L across the two rolls — the Mar-27/Mar-28 SOFR steepener and this one — is **+$437.50**. Worth noting where this pair ended up: the relative kept going after we were out, reaching −9.0bps by 4 September, with the asymmetry now sitting in the euro leg rather than the UK one. Whatever the next expression is, it is not short the UK against Europe.
